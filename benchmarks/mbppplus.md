## Instrument Overview
MBPP+ is the EvalPlus rebuild of MBPP, adding roughly 35 times more automatically generated tests and differential testing against a re-implemented reference. It exists because the original benchmarks accepted incorrect programs: EvalPlus found over 10% of HumanEval ground-truth implementations defective and showed that augmented testing reduced reported pass rates by up to 28.9% in relative terms. It measures functional correctness of short standalone Python functions.

## Supported Uses
- Detecting solutions that pass thin reference tests but are functionally wrong
- Regression-testing a single model or decoding configuration against a fixed harness version
- Establishing a floor for basic Python function synthesis alongside a contamination-controlled benchmark

## Unsupported Inferences
- Ranking frontier models from pass@1 differences below roughly seven points
- Reading a high MBPP+ score as general or real-world programming ability
- Comparing scores across MBPP+ dataset versions, which changed task counts and reference solutions

## Measurement Targets
- Functional correctness of short standalone Python functions
- Robustness of generated code to edge-case inputs
- Test adequacy relative to the original MBPP suite
- Greedy-decoding pass@1 behavior

## Evidence History
- 2023: EvalPlus was published at NeurIPS with HumanEval+ and a stated plan to extend to MBPP.
- 2024: MBPP+ v0.2.0 removed broken tasks, cutting the suite from 399 to 378 and shifting pass@1 by about four points.
- 2024: Reference solutions for three MBPP+ tasks were corrected after external error reports.
- 2024: EvoEval, from the same authors, reported an average 39.4% performance drop and large ranking changes when problems were transformed.
- 2026: The official leaderboard's newest entries remain from late 2024, leaving current frontier models unrepresented.

## Validity Threats
- [The maintainers' own release notes record that MBPP+ shipped with broken tasks for roughly a year, that removing them cut the suite from 399 to 378 tasks with an expected four-point pass@1 improvement, and that ground-truth solutions for three further tasks were corrected after external reports.](https://github.com/evalplus/evalplus)
- [An independent noise analysis of the EvalPlus family manually inspected the ten most suspect MBPP and MBPP+ examples and found seven genuinely wrong, and calculated that a 164-item benchmark of this type requires at least a 6.7 point difference to reach a p-value of 0.05 across pairwise comparisons.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21326)
- [Transforming EvalPlus-style problems produced an average 39.4% performance drop across 51 models, ranging from 19.6% to 47.7%, with drastic ranking changes that the authors attribute to overfitting of existing benchmarks.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19114)
- [A contamination study matching popular code-generation benchmarks against open pretraining corpora found substantial overlap and showed that models perform significantly better on the subset of problems whose similar solutions appeared in training data.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04811)
- [A time-segmented competition benchmark reported a Pearson correlation of only 0.72 between HumanEval+ pass@1 and its own easy split, with one 1.3B model scoring 59.8% on HumanEval+ but 26.3% on the easy split, and identified the divergent group as finetuned variants of open-access models.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07974)
- [A decade-scale survey of 672 code benchmarks found that in 2025 alone the number of benchmarks ignoring code coverage when providing test cases nearly matched the total accumulated across the previous ten years.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10711)
